First, the framing. Bear markets are not random price events. They are wealth transfers. The same way a bank run isn't a financial event, it's a coordination event where panicked depositors hand their balances to whoever stays calm.

Crypto bear markets follow the same shape. The supply of coins doesn't change. The number of holders doesn't change much either. What changes is the distribution. Who holds what coins, at what cost basis. Every bear, that distribution shifts dramatically toward the holders who don't sell.

90/10 is approximate. Sometimes it's 80/20, sometimes 95/5. The point is the ratio is always lopsided.

Second, what happened in Oct/Nov.

The four-year cycle zombies dumped. Long-term holders distributed (the on-chain data is unambiguous). Retail panicked. Every wave of selling created a discount for whoever was on the other side of the trade.

The four-year cycle is dead. I've made that case in other posts. But the dumpers didn't know that. They saw the calendar, they saw a shutdown-driven dip, they assumed the bear had arrived. They sold into the assumption.

Whoever didn't sell got the discount.

Third, the cost. If the four-year cycle zombies hadn't dumped, $BTC would be at roughly $95k right now instead of $80k.

That ~$15k delta is the most measurable wealth transfer of the cycle so far. Every dollar of price impact from the dumping went directly to the cost basis of whoever held through it. The dumpers paid $15k per Bitcoin to learn that their thesis was wrong. The holders received $15k per Bitcoin for being right.

Whoever is right about the macro stays. Whoever is wrong leaves. The price discounts the leaving until the right side starts buying.

This is not unique to crypto. Real estate in 2008. Tech stocks in 2002. The mechanism is the same.
