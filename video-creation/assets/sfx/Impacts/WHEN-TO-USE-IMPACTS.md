Reference for placing impact hits (the `Impacts/` files: `Impact_1/2/3.wav`, `Impact_Hit_01*.wav`,
`Kick_Impact_01.wav`, `Soundjay_Impact_Main_01.wav`, `DSGNImpt-single_impact...mp3`). Captured from

`transition_rapid_whoosh.mp3`, `Boom - Big Reveal.wav`).

## Best times to fire an impact

- **Physical contact / action** — right on a physical hit: a punch landing, a heavy door slamming,
  an object dropped on a surface.
- **On-screen graphic reveals** — exactly when a title card, lower-third, or logo pops onto screen,
  to give the text visual weight.
- **Dynamic transitions** — on the exact frame you cut to a striking new scene; makes the visual
  change feel deliberate and energetic. (For us: the chapter-transition cut, big b-roll punches.)
- **Emphasis (vibe cuts)** — pair an impact (boom / bass drop) with an abrupt music stop and a crash
  zoom to emphasize an extremely important point.

## Pro tips

- **Avoid overuse** — sound design thrives on subtlety. An impact on every edit loses its shock value
  and fatigues the audience. Reserve them for the beats that actually matter.
- **Build the anticipation** — an impact sounds much larger after a build-up. Layer a riser or a
  whoosh to build tension right before the hit lands.
- **Layer for realism** — don't rely on one raw file. Combine layers (sub-bass drop + metallic thud +
  crisp high-end element) for a fully fleshed-out hit.

## How this maps to our edited-video kit

- Impact on each **chapter-transition** cut (the one chapter transition we pick), optionally preceded
  by a whoosh/riser for the build-up. Keep it consistent so it reads as the video's "punctuation."
- Impact on **major graphic reveals** only (title cards, the corrected how-it-works diagram, code
  card reveal, timeline strip), not on every container/b-roll change.
- Reserve the **vibe-cut** combo (impact + music drop + crash zoom) for the 1-2 biggest conviction
  beats per video (e.g. the hook payoff, the close).
