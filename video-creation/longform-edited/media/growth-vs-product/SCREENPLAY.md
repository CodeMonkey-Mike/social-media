## THE HOOK / THESIS

Companies now post two titles next to "AI engineer": **AI growth engineer** (CrowdStrike, MISUMI Americas via Fictiv, Arize AI, a Kforce client; DATA.md T2) and **AI product engineer** (Citian, GovWell, Brain Co., Clera, Mimic; T3). The video takes the AI off both titles first. A growth engineer pushes the product out to the customers, gets them onboarded, gets them to stay and grows the actual business (Mike's own framing of the base role; the nouns are supported by The Pragmatic Engineer 2025-03-20, "growth engineering is the writing of code to make a company money", the team metric "typically related to conversion rate or retention", T5, and by the Anthropic growth posting "user acquisition, engagement, retention, and monetization"). A product engineer builds out the features of the actual product for the customer and does not market it (Mike's framing; The Pragmatic Engineer, receipt C12: "Product Engineers focus on creating a Product worth paying for ... Growth Engineers instead focus on making that good product have a good business"; Citian "product-minded software engineer who turns customer problems into reliable AI-enabled capabilities", T6). Then it puts AI in front of both titles: the AI growth engineer moves the same business number with AI (landing pages, onboarding and retention personalized for every user, the experiments that prove it; every growth posting opened sits inside marketing or go-to-market, Pillar 1 summary), the AI product engineer builds the AI inside the feature (evals, grounding, latency, cost; every product posting opened, Pillar 1 summary). In Mike's experience the two titles get used interchangeably, including by the people hiring for them (his observation, never a statistic; T4). They are not the same job. Both ask for the same technical vocabulary: LLM APIs, agents, RAG, orchestration, Python or TypeScript (T7). Same tools, different scoreboard. Three differences carry the body: the scoreboard (what each is measured on), the clock (speed vs durability) and the bias (what each reaches for first), then which one to hire first and which one to become. Company names are carried by the receipts on screen; the VO speaks none of them.
