### Pillar 2: THE SCOREBOARD (what each role is measured on)

**Growth side (business number, experiments, attribution):**
- "Essentially, growth engineering is the writing of code to make a company money." and the team metric is "a key business metric, which differs by team but is typically related to conversion rate or retention" and "the more A/B tests a team can run in a given quarter, the more of them will end up winners, making the team successful." (Orosz and Komissarouk, The Pragmatic Engineer, 2025-03-20, https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/what-is-growth-engineering, read 2026-10-20.)
- "Growth engineers' work lives or dies by the customer impact it has." and "To measure the impact of our changes, we experiment through A/B testing." (Andrew Hammond, Inside Atlassian, 2020-05-21, https://www.atlassian.com/engineering/what-does-a-product-growth-engineer-work-on, read 2026-10-20.)
- "[W]e already covered that they work to increase signups, subscriptions, and revenue by working across the product." (Ian Vanagas, PostHog, 2023-04-26, https://posthog.com/blog/what-is-a-growth-engineer, read 2026-10-20.)
- Posting language: Fictiv/MISUMI "funnel performance, segment health, and campaign attribution"; Recruiting from Scratch "tracking and attribution systems that help Growth, Sales, and Marketing understand what is driving results"; Anthropic "A/B tests and experiments to optimize user onboarding, feature adoption, subscription conversion"; CrowdStrike "Success in this role is measured not only by what you ship, but by workflows permanently transformed, adoption that sticks" (sources in Pillar 1).
- The pirate-metrics funnel the growth side counts in: Dave McClure's "Startup Metrics for Pirates: AARRR !!!" deck names Acquisition ("users come to the site from various channels"), Activation ("users enjoy 1st visit: 'happy' user experience"), Retention ("users come back, visit site multiple times"), Referral ("users like product enough to refer others"), Revenue ("users conduct some monetization behavior"). (https://www.slideshare.net/dmc500hats/startup-metrics-for-pirates-long-version, read 2026-10-20; the deck shows no year, see do-not-air.)

**Product side (the feature's own quality):**
- Hamel Husain, 2024-03-29: "I've found that unsuccessful products almost always share a common root cause: a failure to create robust evaluation systems." Three levels: Level 1 unit tests (fast assertions on each code change), Level 2 human and model eval (reviewing logged traces by hand and with LLM judges), Level 3 A/B testing (experiments with real users to confirm the product is "driving user behaviors or outcomes you desire"). (https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals/, read 2026-10-20.) Note for the strategist: Level 3 is where the two scoreboards touch; the product engineer's A/B test is about the feature, the growth engineer's is about the funnel.
- Anthropic docs, "Define success criteria and build evaluations", the non-exhaustive criteria list: Task fidelity ("How well does the model need to perform on the task?"), Consistency, Relevance and coherence, Tone and style, Privacy preservation, Context utilization, Latency ("What is the acceptable response time for the model?"), Price ("What is your budget for running the model? Consider factors like the cost for each API call, the size of the model, and the frequency of usage."). Good criteria are "Specific", "Measurable", "Achievable", "Relevant". (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/define-success, read 2026-10-20.)
- Posting language: Citian "correctness, grounding, authorization, appropriate abstention and regression detection"; Clera "model accuracy, hallucinations, retrieval quality, latency, and cost"; GovWell "hallucination detection and answer-quality scoring, tracked against explicit pass-rate targets"; Mimic "reliability, latency, scalability, and production correctness" (sources in Pillar 1).

**Worked scenario (mirrors the 2023 "tweet vs podcast" beat; every element traces to a posting):**
- Easy to attribute: an AI-personalized landing page or offer in a split test. Fictiv/MISUMI: "Build the experimentation infrastructure that lets the team test messaging, offers, and channel mix"; Fictiv: "Build personalization logic for inbound and outbound programs". Atlassian: "a control group keeps the standard experience while a variation group sees the new one". The number moves or it does not; kill it or scale it.
- Hard to attribute: AI-generated developer content and community presence. Arize's AI Growth Engineer posting IS that job: "engage the AI community through AI content on a daily basis" and "turn consumption of AI content into OSS product usage & awareness". No posting opened names an attribution method for it, which is the point of the beat.
- The product engineer's version of "attribution" is a trace, not a channel: Citian "Make AI-generated results traceable to their underlying data, queries and assumptions."

### Pillar 3: THE CLOCK (speed vs durability)

**Growth engineers run many cheap experiments against live traffic:**
- "Growth engineers care more about real results from experiments than designing the perfect experiment." and "Since growth engineers run many experiments, they must be able to craft hypotheses and evaluate results." (PostHog, 2023-04-26.)
- "Growth teams aim to get to the earliest possible signal of a new feature's performance." and "it is rare for a project to span longer than three months." and a failed experiment still counts because "the outcome of this process is learning, and that's what we aim for." (Atlassian, 2020-05-21.)
- Elena Verna, as quoted by The Pragmatic Engineer (2025-03-20): "Product Engineering teams ship to build; Growth Engineering teams ship to learn." (Secondhand quote: air with "as quoted in The Pragmatic Engineer" or not at all; see Open question 4.)
- Sean Ellis on the speed stereotype, in his own voice (Lenny's Podcast, 2024-09-05, 46:10 to 46:27): "when I coined growth hacking, I did not think of it as a bunch of one-off hacks. What I thought of it was what's more about what is the way to ultimately drive sustainable growth?" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VjJ6xcv7e8s, uploader captions, read 2026-10-20.) Useful for the stereotype line: the coiner himself rejects "fast hacks".
- Posting language for the fast loop: Recruiting from Scratch "Form clear hypotheses, ship variants, measure results, and iterate based on observed user behavior"; CrowdStrike "you would rather ship a working v1, learn quickly, and iterate than spend a quarter talking about what might be possible".

**Product engineers build the slow loop (evals, guardrails, retrieval quality, upgrades without regressions):**
- Hamel's three levels (above): unit tests on every change, trace review with LLM judges, then A/B. "unsuccessful products almost always share a common root cause: a failure to create robust evaluation systems."
- Hamel on vibe checks, in his own voice (Lenny's Podcast, 2025-09-25, 6:57 to 7:27): "before evals you would be left with guessing you would maybe fix a prompt and hope that you're not breaking anything else with that prompt and you might rely on vibe checks which is totally fine and vibe checks are good and you should do vibe checks initially, but it can become very unmanageable very fast because as your application grows, it's really hard to rely on vibe checks. You just feel lost." (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BsWxPI9UM4c, auto-captions, read 2026-10-20.)
- Posting language: Citian "regression gates, and catching wrong answers before customers do" and "Establish practical release criteria and use production feedback to improve quality"; GovWell "Use real production usage data, not synthetic benchmarks, to decide what to fix next" and "Closing the gap between 'good today' and 'reviewers trust it completely' is real, immediate work"; Clera "turn experiments into dependable systems and features, and build ways to measure and improve their quality".

**Worked scenario (mirrors the 2023 "conversion rate already high" beat):**
- The growth move this week: an AI-personalized outbound or paid loop. Recruiting from Scratch: "Develop automation tooling for personalized outreach at scale"; Clera GTM: "signal-based sequences using indicators such as intent, job changes, and funding activity"; Fictiv: "translate pipeline goals into automated campaign workflows". Target met, number moves, then it is scrapped or scaled.
- The product move over the same weeks: an eval set and a retrieval fix. GovWell: "Improve AI Community Assistant's retrieval grounding and evaluation infrastructure so its answers hold up as we expand to more jurisdictions"; Citian: "test sets, correctness and grounding measures, regression gates". The eval set is reused on every model upgrade (Anthropic docs, "Achievable: Base your targets on industry benchmarks, prior experiments, AI research, or expert knowledge").
- One-line contrast card: GROWTH ENGINEER / rapid results, kill or scale this week; PRODUCT ENGINEER / compounding quality, the eval set outlives the model (wording for the strategist; the facts above support it, the phrasing is not a quote).

### Pillar 4: THE BIAS (the two tool stacks; the only diagram)

**Growth engineer stack, every node from a posting (read 2026-10-20):**
- GTM and marketing systems: "work management platforms (e.g., Workfront, Asana), marketing automation systems (e.g., Marketo, HubSpot), CMS platforms" (CrowdStrike); "CDP, marketing automation, CRM, and experimentation tooling" (Fictiv/MISUMI); "marketing automation platforms, CDPs, and CRM ecosystems" (Kforce); "Webflow", "forms, gated content, event tracking, and attribution" (Cresta); "Clay", "n8n, Make, or Zapier", "Pipedrive" (Clera GTM Engineer); "Reddit, LinkedIn, and legal communities", "analytics dashboards" (Recruiting from Scratch).
- Agent and content pipelines glued to GTM: "AI APIs, MCP servers, automation platforms, and enterprise systems into our GTM motion"; "HTML/email template generation from agent outputs, design asset pipelines (PPTX, PDF, SVG), and content publishing workflows"; "LangChain, CrewAI, Claude Code agents"; "campaign dashboards, workflow builders, approval interfaces" (CrowdStrike).
- Data and experiments: "experimentation frameworks", "A/B testing", "attribution" (Kforce); "holdouts, bandits, and off-policy estimates where A/B tests aren't practical" (Anthropic "Data Scientist, Growth", https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/anthropic/jobs/5161402008, first published 2026-03-23).
- Uniquely good at (sourced): translating "marketing business problems into technical designs and working applications" (CrowdStrike); building "the tooling that lets marketers ship without an engineer in the loop" (Cresta).

**Product engineer stack, every node from a posting (read 2026-10-20):**
- Model and retrieval: "prompting, tool use, retrieval, text-to-SQL, fine-tuning or no AI at all" (Citian); "Jurisdiction-aware retrieval", "Multi-model orchestration across Google Gemini and OpenAI, routed by task" (GovWell); "retrieval, drafting, document, and agent workflows", "vector databases" (Clera).
- Quality loop: "Custom evaluation harness for hallucination detection and answer-quality scoring, tracked against explicit pass-rate targets" (GovWell); "evaluation pipelines, benchmarks" with "dashboards that make changes in system performance visible" (Clera); "test sets, correctness and grounding measures, regression gates" (Citian).
- Production and UX for a model that is wrong sometimes: "latency, cost, reliability, monitoring, failure modes and incident response", "appropriate abstention", "guard against prompt injection", "Enforce customer isolation and each user's own permissions" (Citian); "a system reliable enough that a government reviewer will actually trust its output" (GovWell); app stack "RedwoodJS React TypeScript Node.js GraphQL PostgreSQL AWS" (GovWell), "FastAPI, Docker, React, Next.js, Postgres" (Clera).
- Uniquely good at (sourced): knowing "the difference between a convincing demo and a dependable product" and "when AI is the right approach and when conventional software is better" (Citian).

**Overlap nodes (both stacks; for the diagram's shared middle):** LLM APIs ("Claude, GPT, or similar models at scale", CrowdStrike; "shipped LLM features used by customers", Citian), agents and orchestration (both), RAG (CrowdStrike bonus; Clera, GovWell core), evals (CrowdStrike "evaluation-driven development"; every product posting), Python plus TypeScript (CrowdStrike "8+ years of full-stack engineering experience with Python and modern JavaScript or TypeScript"; Citian "Python or TypeScript back ends").

### Pillar 5: which one to hire, which one to become

- **Company stage, what the postings show (no percentages, just the opened set):** the product title was posted by small, venture-backed product companies (Citian 11-50 employees; GovWell founded 2023, $35M raised; Mimic $77M raised; Clera healthtech; Brain Co.), and the AI growth title by large or established marketing organisations (CrowdStrike 5,001-10,000; MISUMI Group's Americas division; Anthropic's growth team for the adjacent title) and by a staffing firm's client. One live counter-example to "growth is only big-company": the Recruiting from Scratch legal-AI company has "fewer than 20 engineers today" and is hiring growth engineering. Airable line: a company with a product users already touch hires the product engineer first; a company with a product that already works and a funnel to feed hires the growth engineer; the sources do not support a universal rule.
- **The overlap, in the postings' own words:** the growth posting demands evals ("Experience using evaluation frameworks for AI systems and applying evaluation-driven development", CrowdStrike); the product posting demands experiments ("continuously improve AI experiences through experimentation, evaluation, and rapid iteration", Mimic; "prototype quickly, validate both the value and the correctness of what you build, and turn the experiments that work into production software", Citian); both read data (CrowdStrike "regression tests, and monitoring to measure solution quality, reliability, and business outcomes"; GovWell "Use real production usage data").
- **Titles drift (sourced examples):** "AI Infrastructure / Product Engineer" (Clera); "Product Engineer (AI)" (Mimic); "Staff Product Engineer, Agentic Identity & Governance, AI Platform" (Redpanda Data, LinkedIn 4471259170, radar 2026-10-20); "AI Application Engineer + Product Engineer" as one Hacker News ad (Typify.dev, radar 2026-10-13: "Forward-deployed, ship LLM features close to real users, iterate on real usage, design guardrails and evals"); "GTM Engineer" doing growth engineering (Coframe, Clera); Mike's own LinkedIn headline "Crypto Educator & AI Growth Engineer" (applications.json, `linkedin-automation/private/data/`, read 2026-10-20; see Open question 6).
- **"Founding AI engineer is usually both":** no posting with that exact title was opened for this file; Mike's radar queries "founding AI engineer" and the find-jobs skill lists it as a family (find-jobs.md line 70). Air it as Mike's experience in conditional language, not as a sourced fact (Open question 7).
- **The "become which" line, sourced on the growth side:** Sean Ellis's definition applies to the person, not the tools: "A growth hacker is a person whose true north is growth." (2010-07-26.) On the product side, swyx: "AI Engineers should certainly view product-specific data and evals as their job." (2023-06-30.)
