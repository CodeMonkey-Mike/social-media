## THE HOOK / THESIS

The "safe job" advice has already failed once: a decade of "learn to code" (President Obama's Hour of Code message, 2013), and then AI learned to code (75 percent of Google's new code is AI-generated). The standard career advice of the AI era is "learn a trade": Geoffrey Hinton said "Train to be a plumber" (2025-06-16) and Jensen Huang said the world will need hundreds of thousands of electricians, plumbers and carpenters (September 2025). Hinton's own sentence ends "Until the humanoid robots show up", and that clause is the turn of this video. The brain went first: employers blamed AI for 120,136 announced US job cuts in the first nine months of 2026, and young workers in AI-exposed jobs are about 19 percent behind. Then it got a body: about 13,000 humanoids shipped in 2025, one held a factory job at BMW for 11 months, and a home humanoid is on sale for 20 thousand dollars. No robot does residential trade work yet; the script says so once, upfront, and the rest is Mike's forecast (T3) that it is coming within a few years, argued from the fact that a house is built for a human body, so one machine with that body can use every tool in it. The same four-part test (better, faster, cheaper, safer; from the reference, said as Mike's own) runs up the pay ladder to the neurosurgeon at about 830 thousand dollars a year, where autonomy so far is 17 steps on pig tissue. If the forecast plays out, prices fall where machines do the work, paychecks disappear, industries survive while the jobs inside them do not, and working people lose their only leverage. The answer, held as an open loop until CH7, is to convert a paycheck into ownership of what stays scarce and what nobody controls while the paycheck still exists; that is Mike's conditional case for crypto in an AI world. Per persona, none of this is spoken up front as a summary.
