**The historical analogs, re-sourced.**
- **Telephone operators.** "In the early 1900s, telephone operation was among the most common jobs for American women." Between 1920 and 1940 AT&T replaced operators "with mechanical switching technology in over half of the U.S. telephone network." Automation "eliminated most of these jobs". The next cohorts of young women found other work (clerical and service jobs), but "incumbent telephone operators were most impacted, and a decade later more likely to be in lower-paying occupations or no longer working." (Feigenbaum and Gross, NBER Working Paper 28061, November 2020, revised February 2024, https://www.nber.org/papers/w28061, read 2026-10-06). Use it honestly: this is the case where new jobs DID appear for the next generation, and the people already in the job still lost.
