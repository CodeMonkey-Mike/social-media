**MIKE'S THOUGHTS (the spine of the script; his own dictated words, lightly cleaned; keep his argument and his order of ideas, deepen it with DATA.md facts):**

1. **The thesis.** Software engineers need to be more business-minded and more entrepreneurial. You can no longer keep your head down and just code, because it's not going to work out. You can no longer be an engineer who doesn't want to adopt AI. You have to do more than code.
2. **Why now.** AI can do most of the work, most of the code. A lot of AI engineers are not necessarily coding anymore. Mike's framing: "we've reached a point of recursive self-improvement where the AIs can improve themselves." Most code at Anthropic is being written by AI, and Google says 75% of its code is written by AI (BOTH claims: verify the exact wording, number, speaker and date from primary sources; if the 75% figure is not what Google said, DATA.md gives the true latest figure and the screenplay uses it). We're at the end of 2026; imagine the end of 2027, 12 months from now. There will be less and less demand for software engineers who only want to sit there and code.
3. **The old pod vs the new one.** Engineers typically work in a pod inside their company, on one or more steps of the funnel. In the past a pod might have been a frontend engineer, a backend engineer, a QA engineer, a project manager, maybe a designer and a marketer. Now one person can fill almost all of those roles.
4. **The versions of the software engineer now:**
   - **Growth engineer.** Has existed for a while: the combination of a growth marketer and a software engineer (sometimes frontend, sometimes full-stack). Knows all six steps of the pirate funnel, **AAARRR** (Awareness, Acquisition, Activation, Retention, Referral, Revenue; the three-A funnel, see the persona note below) and works in one or more of them. Takes the role of the growth marketer AND the growth hacker: in the past a growth hacker was a slightly more technical growth marketer; the growth hacker is about tactics, shorter-term growth and rapid turnaround, the growth marketer about strategy and the bigger picture. A growth engineer has all those traits, plus they're a software engineer. Focus: getting the product out there and marketing it to the market.
   - **Product engineer** (some call it a feature engineer). Mostly concerned with features, what the customer interacts with in the product; a lot of overlap with the growth engineer. Has to care about the customer and what the customer needs, and above all about RETENTION: in pirate-funnel terms, getting the customer to keep using the product.
   - **Platform engineer.** Maybe not doing anything related to marketing or the product: works on something internal, in the systems, the backend of the company. Again can fill a lot of roles. There could be a PM for those teams that work on something internal.
   - **GTM engineer.** An engineer with go-to-market experience who deals more with sales: a combination of someone who works with sales and a software engineer. Some overlap with the growth engineer, but works in different systems (Salesforce and so on).
5. **Who adapts and who falls behind.** Younger people, teenagers today who have yet to enter the market, will enter it with this mindset and it will be easier for them to adapt. Some of the others already in the market don't want to believe in the change, and they're going to fall behind.
