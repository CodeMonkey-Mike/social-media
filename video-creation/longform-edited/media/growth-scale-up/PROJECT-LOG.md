**Opening thesis (every claim verified in DATA.md before it airs):** the head-of-growth job used to scale with headcount: the optimization stage meant a team of 7 to 12 people running a high-tempo testing program. With AI agents, a team of one or five can run that tempo. The old move when you got budget was to list the work you don't like and hire a junior to do it, which buys no leverage. The new move: hand the low-impact, repeatable work to agents, and spend every human hour, yours and your reports', on the highest-impact work. Fewer people, higher velocity.

**The spine of the video = it ALTERNATES between two seats, every pillar shown from both:**
- **TEAM OF ONE (solo growth engineer):** you are the strategist, the executor AND the leader. Your agents are your team; you direct them.
- **TEAM OF FIVE (growth manager, small team):** player-coach, half hands-on, half leading; your direct reports each run their own agents, and your job includes how THEY use AI.

**Pillars (each needs DATA.md facts; the researcher says so if one cannot be sourced):**
1. **The fork: hire for the work you hate, or hand it to agents.** Capland's 6:51 fork, updated: the low-impact list now goes to agents (or is stopped altogether), and headcount, when it comes, is hired into the highest-impact work. Solo seat: what that list looks like in AARRR terms (variant copy, weekly funnel readout, UTM / event hygiene, lifecycle email drafts, competitor and review scraping). Team seat: each report does the same for their own list.
2. **When to use AI, and what for (Mike flagged this as a key talking point).** A simple split: STRATEGY = human judgment (what matters, which bets, what to stop), AI only as a sparring partner; EXECUTION = agents (build variants, draft, instrument, ship); ANALYSIS = agents draft, a human decides "scrap or scale". The anti-pattern from Capland 14:18: the 11-page AI strategy brief handed to the manager, AI used as a senior employee instead of as leverage. Solo seat: you hold the strategy. Team seat: you write the guardrails.
3. **One-on-ones and operating principles, now for humans AND agents.** Capland 10:34: 1:1s transfer judgment, not status. The AI angle: the status update is the agent's job now (the agent writes the weekly funnel readout), so the whole 1:1 goes to judgment transfer. And the same operating principles (speed vs rigor, polish vs rough draft, 13:18) get WRITTEN DOWN as agent instructions, because an agent only knows the trade-offs you put in writing. Worked example from OUR OWN pipeline (the researcher reads the repo and cites paths, nothing invented): this repo runs a solo content operation through versioned agent definitions in `.claude/agents/` (count them) and per-folder `CLAUDE.md` / `SKILL.md` files, i.e. Capland's "growth operations manual" (16:51) written for agents; the orchestrator gates (HITL) are where the human keeps the judgment. Keep it a one-line receipt, the video is about growth, not about this repo.
4. **Strategy and direction (12:30): clarify and verify.** Solo seat: the strategy artifact (what matters most, KPIs, what you say "not right now" to) is ALSO the context file every agent reads. Team seat: clarify and verify with the people, and the same doc keeps their agents aligned.
5. **Optimization and scale-up mode (22:40).** The stage that used to require a 7 to 12 person team running high-volume experiments and segmentation is where AI changes the headcount math most: experiment throughput is now bounded by judgment and traffic, not by hands. Careful: statistical traffic limits still apply, AI does not make a low-traffic test significant; one honest line on that.
6. **AT LEAST TWO plays NOT in Capland's video (Mike asked for these; the researcher sources each tool / capability from primary docs, current as of 2026-10, and picks the strongest two or three):**
   a. **The AI experiment loop (acquisition / activation):** agents mine the funnel data and session recordings for drop-off, draft a hypothesis backlog scored (ICE / PIE), generate the landing-page or onboarding variants, and write the readout; the human picks the bets and makes the scrap-or-scale call. Candidate primary sources: GrowthBook / Statsig / Optimizely / Amplitude / PostHog AI features, Microsoft Clarity Copilot session summaries.
   b. **The retention / churn agent:** an agent watches product-usage events + support tickets, flags accounts showing churn signals (falling usage, failed payments, negative tickets), and drafts the personalized save or win-back message; the human owns the templates and the offer. Candidate sources: Amplitude / Mixpanel predictive cohorts, Stripe dunning / Smart Retries, Intercom Fin, Customer.io / Braze AI.
