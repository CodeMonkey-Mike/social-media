## 2. Pillar facts

### Pillar 1: The fork, hire for the work you hate or hand it to agents
- **Capland's fork, verbatim (audio, 6:48.8-7:13.2):** "this is kind of where you're facing your first fork in the road. Now there's two ways that you could advocate for a hire. If you're a team of one and you're going to be scaling to two, you could create a list of all the work that you don't like doing and go hire somebody to do that stuff to take it off your plate. That's one option. Another option would be to make a list of the most impactful work that would move the business towards its growth goals and go figure out how someone could spend their time doing that." (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1smUpbXYAuQ, read 2026-10-25)
- **The no-leverage line (audio, 7:23.1-7:33.2):** "And I get why folks are tempted to do this, but the challenge is that you gain no leverage from doing that. The leverage comes from figuring out how do we grow faster and then hiring into that leverage."
- **Stop it altogether (audio, 7:37.6-7:54.8):** "that's low impact work, please don't hire somebody to do it. In fact, maybe you should stop doing it altogether. A huge lever when you're leading any team is to figure out what is the most important work to do and why. And as you take on new impactful work, it's okay to say no or to stop doing or let go of some of that less impactful work."
- **The AARRR frame for the list (Mike's own definition of the role):** Mike defines growth engineering by the pirate funnel: landing-page conversion, acquisition, onboarding / activation, retention, A/B experiments, "scrapped or scaled" (Mike's spoken definition, recorded 2026-10-21 in the growth-vs-product screenplay session; memory note `user_mike_growth_engineering_is_aarrr`). The funnel's five stages, verbatim from Dave McClure's deck "Startup Metrics for Pirates", slide 2 "Customer Lifecycle: 5 Steps to Success": Acquisition "users come to the site from various channels"; Activation "users enjoy 1st visit: 'happy' user experience"; Retention "users come back, visit site multiple times"; Referral "users like product enough to refer others"; Revenue "users conduct some monetization behavior" (https://www.slideshare.net/dmc500hats/startup-metrics-for-pirates-long-version, read 2026-10-25; the deck shows no year, see Do-not-air).
- **What agents take on the AARRR list (each item traces to a vendor doc in pillar 6, so the list card is sourced, not invented):** variant drafting (Optimizely "Variation generation", PostHog AI creates the experiment and its flag variants); the weekly funnel readout (Statsig AI summary, GrowthBook on-stop summary, PostHog AI results summary); session-recording review (Clarity Grouped Session Insights); churn flagging (Amplitude predictive cohorts, Amplitude Custom Agents "churn monitoring" template); failed-payment recovery (Stripe Smart Retries). Items the brief lists that have NO vendor doc opened here and must stay generic or be dropped: "UTM / event hygiene", "lifecycle email drafts", "competitor and review scraping" (Open question 3).
- **Plain-language summary the sources support (for the strategist, not a quote):** Capland's fork is about hiring a human; his own advice for the low-impact list is "stop doing it altogether". Mike's update, in Mike's words: the low-impact list goes to agents (or stops), and the first human hire, when it comes, goes into the highest-impact work. The sources support the fork and the vendor capabilities; the "agents instead of a junior" move is Mike's.

### Pillar 5: Optimization and scale-up mode (the headcount math)
- **The three stages as Capland tells them (audio, 21:50.0-22:00.5):** "And Adam talks about three distinct stages of growth, I think is really applicable to what we're talking about. Stages are building, optimizing, and innovating."
- **BUILD (audio, 22:00.5-22:26.2):** "the building stage of growth, this is really all about going zero to one, making the first versions of everything in our growth model, the first channel decisions and campaigns and email nurture sequence and the onboarding flow and the checkout experience to churn prevention and loss prevention sequences ... typically, this is going to be your solo head of growth, or maybe like up until a growth team of about three people or so."
- **OPTIMIZE (audio, 22:32.8-23:00.6):** quoted in T1: testing, experiments, segmentation, "break through the plateaus", "about seven to 12 people", "emerging out of the ... super early stage startup mode and into the scale up mode".
- **INNOVATE (auto-captions, 23:02-23:26):** "the innovation stage. Meaning we've already done a ton of testing. We've optimized as much as we can. We're hitting the point of diminishing returns. Now we've got to layer on some new stuff ... new channels, new loops, maybe additional products or new features ... this is typically like your 20 plus person growth team".
- **ATTRIBUTION CHECK (load-bearing):** Capland attributes the stages to Adam Fishman ("former head of growth at Lyft. He's at Mozilla today. He's also a Reforge instructor", auto-captions 21:42-21:48). Fishman's OWN writing uses these three words as practitioner ARCHETYPES, not stages: "I identified three archetypes for growth practitioners - painter, architect and surgeon. We generalized these in the Growth Leadership program as the innovator, the builder and the optimizer." and "an Optimizer will need a depth of channel fluency ... because they are in charge of fine-tuning an already-established loop" / "A builder will need depth in experimentation ... because their job is largely setting up growth systems and testing new loops." (https://www.fishmanafnewsletter.com/p/growth-skill-building-and-your-career-phases, 2023-09-19, read 2026-10-25). Fishman's own stage language is funding rounds: Series A "you need to hire a hands-on architect or builder as your first leader"; Series B "Moving from single Growth practitioner to small team" and "optimizers. These are specialists who can turn insights from one channel or area into hundreds of new hypotheses"; Series C+ "Growth leadership at scale" (https://www.fishmanafnewsletter.com/p/stop-messing-up-your-growth-leader-hiring, 2025-03-26, read 2026-10-25). Reforge's own page: "The three common growth practitioner profiles are Builder, Optimizer, and Innovator" and the framework was built "with growth experts Adam Fishman and Elena Verna" (https://www.reforge.com/blog/build-growth-team, 2023-02-21, read 2026-10-25). **So: the archetypes are Fishman's (with Reforge / Verna); the stage framing AND the headcounts (1 to 3, 7 to 12, 20+) are Capland's rendering.** Credit line that is honest: "Andrew Capland, building on Adam Fishman's builder / optimizer / innovator framing".
- **The honest statistics line (AI does not make a low-traffic test significant):** Evan Miller's sample-size calculator defines "The Minimum Detectable Effect is the smallest effect that will be detected (1-β)% of the time" and its worked default shows a baseline of 10.2% lifted to 13.2% needing **2,545 subjects per variation** (https://www.evanmiller.org/ab-testing/sample-size.html, read 2026-10-25; the page does not print its power and alpha defaults, so quote the example, not the parameters). Optimizely's Experiment Planning agent itself returns "statistical sizing guidance" in its plan (pillar 6), i.e. the vendors' agents respect the sample-size limit rather than remove it. PostHog judges significance "based on the confidence intervals". Airable: "AI drafts more tests; it does not give a page with 300 visitors a significant result; the sample-size math is the same".
- **Throughput framing the sources support:** Fishman on optimizers: "specialists who can turn insights from one channel or area into hundreds of new hypotheses" (2025-03-26); Optimizely's Test Ideation agent "generates a prioritized package of experiment ideas for any page". The claim "experiment throughput is now bounded by judgment and traffic, not by hands" is Mike's synthesis; air it as his.
