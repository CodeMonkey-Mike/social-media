### Pillar 2a: ARBITRUM (ARB), the focus, explained from zero

- **(a) What it is, in one line (Arbitrum's own words):** "a finance-native blockchain platform" that "executes transactions offchain while Ethereum handles settlement and keeps the data available." Ethereum is "the parent chain, also called layer 1"; an Arbitrum chain that settles to Ethereum is "a child chain, also called layer 2". "A Rollup orders many transactions into a batch" and posts compressed data plus a state commitment to Ethereum; an assertion is treated "as valid unless someone challenges it through BoLD" (a fraud proof settles disputes, only one honest validator is needed). Arbitrum One is "an Arbitrum Rollup chain"; Arbitrum Nova is "an AnyTrust chain" with weaker trust assumptions and lower fees. Anyone can "launch your own Arbitrum chain and configure its execution, data availability, fee model, governance, and validation" (the dedicated-chain product, formerly branded Orbit). (https://docs.arbitrum.io/welcome/arbitrum-gentle-introduction, read 2026-10-09)
- **Who builds it:** Offchain Labs, founded by Princeton's Ed Felten (former deputy US chief technology officer), Steven Goldfeder (CEO) and Harry Kalodner (CTO); the technology was licensed from Princeton; Arbitrum One opened to the public on 2021-08-31 after a developer beta from May 2021; Series B USD 100 million led by Lightspeed at a USD 1.2 billion valuation (TechCrunch, 2021-08-31, https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/31/offchain-labs-raises-120-million-to-hide-ethereums-shortcomings-with-arbitrum-scaling-product, read 2026-10-09). Goldfeder then: "Rollups give you the security derived from Ethereum but a much better experience in terms of costs." Arbitrum One and Nova are operated by the Arbitrum Foundation; the chain's treasury is governed by the ArbitrumDAO, whose voters are ARB holders (docs.arbitrum.io above; docs.arbitrum.foundation below).
