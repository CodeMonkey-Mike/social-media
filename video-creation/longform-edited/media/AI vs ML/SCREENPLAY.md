## THE HOOK / THESIS

AI engineer and ML engineer sound like one job and are two. The AI engineer ships products on a model someone else trained (Huyen, DATA T1; Karpathy, T2); the ML engineer builds, trains and deploys the model itself (Stripe Radar posting). The money gap is real and it runs TOWARD the training side: on Levels.fyi US total comp, the top 10 percent of ML engineers start at 495K against a median AI engineer at 155.5K, a gap of 339.5K (DATA T4, framing a). The hook states the size of the gap and withholds its direction; CH3 pays it off. The verdict: the biggest checks sit with the few who train models (and they compete with PhDs), the open door sits with AI engineers (LinkedIn Jobs on the Rise 2026, number 1 in the US), so for a software developer, AI engineering is the way in.

> [!WARNING]
> **DO NOT AIR "AI engineers make 350K more than ML engineers".** No source supports it; every source has the gap running the other way (DATA do-not-air row 1). Airable instead: "the 340 thousand dollar gap goes to the people who train the models."

> [!WARNING]
> **DO NOT AIR "350K" as a sourced number, and do not present the gap as median vs median.** The sourced results are 339.5K (Levels.fyi US, ML 90th percentile minus AI median) and 340K (Anthropic posted midpoints). Median vs median is only 132K. Airable: "around 340 thousand dollars a year, at the top", with the framing printed on screen every time the number shows.

> [!WARNING]
> **DO NOT AIR "ML engineers need a PhD", "AI engineers never train models", "AI engineers are future-proof", or "AI engineer beats ML engineer as the number 1 job".** The ML postings opened state no PhD; Huyen says you may later host or fine-tune your own model; "future-proof" is opinion; LinkedIn's entry lumps the two titles. Airable: "you're competing with PhDs" · "I have never trained a model" (Mike's own work) · "LinkedIn's fastest-growing job in the US for 2026 is AI engineer" (no head-to-head claim).

> [!WARNING]
