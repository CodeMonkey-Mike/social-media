# Topic-Finding Playbook

A starting point for finding better video topics. Built from reverse-engineering a Kaspa
channel's "Marathon Digital + Kaspa" video (youtu.be/M7URqXUz_ME), which had a great topic.

---

## The core insight

**She didn't invent the topic. She reacted to a claim already trending in the community, then
verified it against primary sources.**

- The **narrative gives you the topic** (free, inbound, already has attention).
- The **primary source gives you the content + credibility** (your edge over everyone repeating
  the rumor).

You already have conviction and a community. What's missing is this *inbound* lane: letting the
feed hand you topics instead of pulling them out of your own head every stream.

---

## Case study: how that Marathon Digital video was built

**Topic source:** a claim spreading in the Kaspa community: "Marathon Digital (MARA, the big public
Bitcoin miner) is the largest corporate Kaspa miner / a Trump trust endorsed Kaspa."

**Where she got the actual info (all shown on screen):**
1. **SEC filing (primary source).** Marathon's Form 8-K (Feb 26, 2025). She found and highlighted
   the one line: "...as of Dec 31, 2024, we sold ~$17M [in] Kaspa..." → proof MARA treats Kaspa as
   a *funding source it sells*, not a conviction hold. Most people spreading the claim never read it.
2. **On-chain explorer wallet tags.** The whole bull case rested on "a wallet tag nobody officially
   confirmed" — she checked the Kaspa explorer.
3. **Holdings trackers / dashboards.** A MARA BTC-holdings/purchases dashboard.
4. **The project roadmap.** Pivoted to the Toccata hard fork as the *real* catalyst.
5. **The originating claim itself.** X / community chatter (the Trump-trust-filing angle, the wallet
   rumor).

**Why this topic worked (4 boxes):**
- Trending right now in her niche.
- A big **named institution** involved (NASDAQ company) → "institutional validation" is catnip.
- **Contested** (bulls vs skeptics) → built-in debate → comments.
- She could **add value others can't** by reading the filing instead of repeating the rumor.

---

## The repeatable method (4 steps)

1. **Monitor the feed, not your own head.** Daily ~10-min skim for "wait, is that true?" claims in
   your niche. Every contested claim is a candidate topic.
2. **Pick the claim with a big name + a contested angle.** Outside names attaching themselves to
   your coin travel the furthest.
3. **Be the person who checks the primary source.** Go to the filing / the explorer / the roadmap.
   "I read the actual filing, here's what it really says" beats any hot take.
4. **End on the catalyst YOU control.** Debunk/contextualize the noise, then redirect to the real
   story (for Kaspa: Toccata, programmability, fair launch). The noise is the hook; your thesis is
   the payoff.

## Where to look (source shortlist)

**Topic radar (where the trending claims live):**
- Kaspa X / Crypto Twitter (big accounts + your replies/mentions)
- r/kaspa, Kaspa Discord/Telegram
- Crypto news aggregators (headlines naming Kaspa or a big entity)
- Your own community's questions and the rumors they bring you

**Primary sources (where you verify, your credibility edge):**
- **SEC EDGAR** (sec.gov/edgar) — search a public company's 8-K / 10-Q / 10-K for "Kaspa". This is
  the move most creators skip.
- **Kaspa block explorer** — wallet tags, balances, flows.
- **CoinGecko / CoinMarketCap** — market cap, listings, holders.
- **The Kaspa roadmap / dev updates** — hard forks (Toccata, DAGKnight), milestones.
- **On-chain dashboards / trackers** — holdings, mining, treasury moves.

---

## Topic-selection filter (is this worth a video?)

A topic is strong if it hits most of these:
- [ ] **Named entity** — a company, fund, exchange, or famous investor attached to the coin.
- [ ] **Already trending** — people are arguing about it *today*.
- [ ] **Contested** — there's a bull side and a skeptic side (debate = comments).
- [ ] **Verifiable** — there's a primary source you can pull that others didn't read.
- [ ] **Ties back to your thesis** — you can redirect to the catalyst you actually care about.

If it hits 3+, it's a video.

---

## A 10-minute morning routine (starting point)

1. Skim Kaspa X + r/kaspa + mentions. Note every "X did/said something about Kaspa" claim.
2. For each, ask: named entity? contested? can I verify it?
3. Pick the 1-3 strongest. For each, find the primary source (EDGAR, explorer, roadmap).
4. Write one line: **the claim**, **what the source actually says**, **the redirect to your thesis**.
5. That line is your video's spine. Walk into the stream with 3-5 of these ready.

## Lane 2: mainstream-media clip reactions

Lane 1 (everything above) is **community narrative** → you verify against a primary source. The topic
comes from *crypto people talking*.

Lane 2 is a different inbound lane: a **mainstream authority figure says something crypto-relevant on a
high-credibility outlet**, and the clip itself is *both* the hook *and* the credibility. The topic comes
from *outsiders and institutions talking about crypto on TV*.

**Case study:** youtu.be/J_quO216dB4 — she found a clip of **Jamie Dimon on Fox Business** and built the
video around reacting to / contextualizing what he said. She didn't make the news; she interpreted a clip
that already carried a big name and a trusted outlet.

**Why Lane 2 works (same 4 boxes as Lane 1):**
- **Named entity** — a CEO / fund manager / official, often from a NASDAQ-tier institution → instant
  "institutional validation."
- **High-credibility outlet** — Fox Business / CNBC / Bloomberg lends authority you don't have to build.
- **Free, reactable B-roll** — you're the *interpreter*, not the source, which is exactly the edge.
- **Predictable** — the same handful of channels and people generate almost all of it, so a watchlist
  turns "lucky find" into a repeatable search.

These are two axes: **where** to look, and **whose name** to search across all of them (the same person

**Channels (business / financial TV with crypto segments):**

**People (search their name + "Bitcoin / crypto / Kaspa / ETF" across the above):**

For each channel's YouTube/X, search **channel + a crypto keyword**, OR search **a name from the people

(the full segment, a filing, on-chain data) so you can add value instead of just replaying the soundbite.
Then end on your own catalyst, same as Lane 1.

## Lane 3: peer-creator topic scouting

A third inbound lane: certain **crypto YouTubers reliably surface great topics every single day**. They've
already done the work of reading the feed and picking what's hot — so their upload schedule is a free,
high-signal topic feed. You're not mining *their take*; you're mining *their topic selection*, then doing
your own primary-source work and Kaspa-thesis redirect on top of it.

**Why this works:** these creators are full-time topic-finders. If you check 3-4 of them each morning,
you inherit their radar for free and almost never walk in topic-less.

**The caveat (same philosophy as Lanes 1 & 2):** surface the topic from them, but don't reproduce their
video. Pull the primary source they skipped, add the angle they can't, end on the catalyst you control.
The peer video is the hook; your thesis is the payoff.

## The Topic Radar dashboard (the tool that runs this playbook)

**Open:** `video-creation/topic-radar/dashboard.html` — fully static, no server, just open the file.
Everything below lives in `video-creation/topic-radar/`.

### The 4 tabs and where each gets its data

| Tab | Lane | Source | Config |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creator Watch List | Lane 3 | YouTube RSS per creator (precise dates + views) | `creators.json` |
| News Clips | Lane 2 | yt-dlp pages ~400 uploads per outlet channel, titles filtered by crypto keywords | `outlets.json` + `CRYPTO_RE` regex in `build-dashboard.js` |
| Crypto Sites | article radar | Site RSS feeds | `sites.json` |
| Policy Radar | regulation | Polymarket search API (odds cards) + cached X posts from policy reporters | `polymarket.json`, `x-accounts.json` |

Adding a source = add one entry to the matching json, rebuild. Yellow **"🎬 Video idea"** cards under
stories come from `video-ideas.json` (news entries match by videoId, article entries by link URL);
they survive rebuilds, so curation is never lost. Title register for ideas: watchlist style — one or
two CAPS power words, an active verb with teeth, a parenthetical second hook, **no em dashes**.

### Refresh procedure (when Mike says "refresh the dashboard / tabs")

1. *(Optional, only if Chrome is free — NEVER while any posting script runs; uses the shared
   xbot-profile)* `python video-creation/topic-radar/fetch-x-policy.py` → recaches X policy posts.
2. `node video-creation/topic-radar/build-dashboard.js` (~2-3 min; `--days N` to widen the window).
   Polymarket odds refresh automatically on every build.
3. **Curation pass** (the human-value step, do not skip): read the new News Clips + Crypto Sites
   items, pick stories that clear the topic-selection filter above, cross-reference against the
   Creator Watch List tab (peer coverage = validation; zero coverage on a big contested story =
   first-mover window), then update `video-ideas.json`: prune idea blocks whose stories aged out,
   add new blocks with 4-5 hype-register titles each. Rebuild once more to render them.

### Known limits (so nobody "fixes" the wrong thing)

- YouTube RSS caps at 15 videos/channel → ultra-prolific creators (Wendy, Heresy) may be undercounted.
- Site RSS feeds cap at ~20-40 items → the Crypto Sites tab really reaches back only ~1.5-3.5 days,
  not the full window. Fast-moving legislative drama can roll out of feeds before a build catches it
  — that's exactly what the Policy Radar tab (X reporters + Polymarket odds) is for.
- News Clips: YouTube sometimes omits upload dates from channel listings; the builder resolves missing
  dates per-video automatically (that's why the news pull is the slow part).
- Yahoo Finance often shows 0 crypto clips — usually genuine, verify before assuming breakage.
- Blockworks (RSS frozen Jan 2026, pivoted to research) and DL News (shut down May 2026) were removed
  from `sites.json` on 2026-07-05 — don't re-add without checking they publish again.
