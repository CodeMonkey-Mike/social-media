# Topic-Finding Playbook

A starting point for finding better video topics. Built from reverse-engineering a Kaspa
channel's "Marathon Digital + Kaspa" video (youtu.be/M7URqXUz_ME), which had a great topic.

---

## The core insight

**She didn't invent the topic. She reacted to a claim already trending in the community, then
verified it against primary sources.**

- The **narrative gives you the topic** (free, inbound, already has attention).
- The **primary source gives you the content + credibility** (your edge over everyone repeating
  the rumor).

You already have conviction and a community. What's missing is this *inbound* lane: letting the
feed hand you topics instead of pulling them out of your own head every stream.

---

## Case study: how that Marathon Digital video was built

**Topic source:** a claim spreading in the Kaspa community: "Marathon Digital (MARA, the big public
Bitcoin miner) is the largest corporate Kaspa miner / a Trump trust endorsed Kaspa."

**Where she got the actual info (all shown on screen):**
1. **SEC filing (primary source).** Marathon's Form 8-K (Feb 26, 2025). She found and highlighted
   the one line: "...as of Dec 31, 2024, we sold ~$17M [in] Kaspa..." → proof MARA treats Kaspa as
   a *funding source it sells*, not a conviction hold. Most people spreading the claim never read it.
2. **On-chain explorer wallet tags.** The whole bull case rested on "a wallet tag nobody officially
   confirmed" — she checked the Kaspa explorer.
3. **Holdings trackers / dashboards.** A MARA BTC-holdings/purchases dashboard.
4. **The project roadmap.** Pivoted to the Toccata hard fork as the *real* catalyst.
5. **The originating claim itself.** X / community chatter (the Trump-trust-filing angle, the wallet
   rumor).

**Why this topic worked (4 boxes):**
- Trending right now in her niche.
- A big **named institution** involved (NASDAQ company) → "institutional validation" is catnip.
- **Contested** (bulls vs skeptics) → built-in debate → comments.
- She could **add value others can't** by reading the filing instead of repeating the rumor.

## The repeatable method (4 steps)

1. **Monitor the feed, not your own head.** Daily ~10-min skim for "wait, is that true?" claims in
   your niche. Every contested claim is a candidate topic.
2. **Pick the claim with a big name + a contested angle.** Outside names attaching themselves to
   your coin travel the furthest.
3. **Be the person who checks the primary source.** Go to the filing / the explorer / the roadmap.
   "I read the actual filing, here's what it really says" beats any hot take.
4. **End on the catalyst YOU control.** Debunk/contextualize the noise, then redirect to the real
   story (for Kaspa: Toccata, programmability, fair launch). The noise is the hook; your thesis is
   the payoff.

## Where to look (source shortlist)

**Topic radar (where the trending claims live):**
- Kaspa X / Crypto Twitter (big accounts + your replies/mentions)
- r/kaspa, Kaspa Discord/Telegram
- Crypto news aggregators (headlines naming Kaspa or a big entity)
- Your own community's questions and the rumors they bring you

**Primary sources (where you verify, your credibility edge):**
- **SEC EDGAR** (sec.gov/edgar) — search a public company's 8-K / 10-Q / 10-K for "Kaspa". This is
  the move most creators skip.
- **Kaspa block explorer** — wallet tags, balances, flows.
- **CoinGecko / CoinMarketCap** — market cap, listings, holders.
- **The Kaspa roadmap / dev updates** — hard forks (Toccata, DAGKnight), milestones.
- **On-chain dashboards / trackers** — holdings, mining, treasury moves.

---

## Topic-selection filter (is this worth a video?)

A topic is strong if it hits most of these:
- [ ] **Named entity** — a company, fund, exchange, or famous investor attached to the coin.
- [ ] **Already trending** — people are arguing about it *today*.
- [ ] **Contested** — there's a bull side and a skeptic side (debate = comments).
- [ ] **Verifiable** — there's a primary source you can pull that others didn't read.
- [ ] **Ties back to your thesis** — you can redirect to the catalyst you actually care about.

If it hits 3+, it's a video.

---

## A 10-minute morning routine (starting point)

1. Skim Kaspa X + r/kaspa + mentions. Note every "X did/said something about Kaspa" claim.
2. For each, ask: named entity? contested? can I verify it?
3. Pick the 1-3 strongest. For each, find the primary source (EDGAR, explorer, roadmap).
4. Write one line: **the claim**, **what the source actually says**, **the redirect to your thesis**.
5. That line is your video's spine. Walk into the stream with 3-5 of these ready.
