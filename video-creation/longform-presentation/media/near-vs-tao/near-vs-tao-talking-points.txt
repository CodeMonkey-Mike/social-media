The angle
A viewer says NEAR is "truly AI" and TAO is just "aggregating crypto subnets." The fun twist: it is close to the opposite. Bittensor's subnets literally make AI (they train models, serve them and rent out the GPUs). NEAR is a blockchain that AI runs on top of (agents, payments, private inference). Both are AI bets, just at different layers of the stack. Stay respectful to the commenter; the point is the framework, not dunking.
Your own position: you hold TAO. Say so up front, it buys trust for the rest.

Slide 2 · Two Different Bets on AI
On screen: two panels, NEAR Protocol (green) and Bittensor (cyan), four points each; NEAR ends on "AI runs on the chain.", TAO on "AI is the product."
NEAR, talking points
    • Started as an AI company. NEAR.ai was founded in 2017 by Illia Polosukhin and Alexander Skidanov to build AI that writes code. They pivoted to blockchain in 2018 because paying their contributors around the world was so hard. Mainnet in 2020, pitched as an Ethereum competitor.
    • The founder pedigree is real. Illia co-wrote "Attention Is All You Need" at Google (2017), the Transformer paper that GPT and ChatGPT are built on. That is the strongest card in the commenter's hand.
    • NEAR AI Cloud: runs open-source models inside secure hardware (Intel TDX plus NVIDIA confidential GPUs), so your prompts stay private, even from the operator. You can stake NEAR and pay for AI with the staking rewards: over 1 million NEAR staked for it (October 2026), 43 models available.
    • NEAR Intents: you say what you want ("swap this for that on another chain") and solvers do it. Built so AI agents can move money across chains. Shade Agents: AI agents with their own multichain wallets and verifiable code.
    • The vision Illia pushed at TOKEN2049 Singapore: privacy by default for AI and finance.
TAO, talking points
    • The whitepaper title says it all: "Bittensor: A Peer-to-Peer Intelligence Market." Intelligence is the commodity.
    • 128 subnets, each one a competition. Miners do the work, validators score it, the best work earns the new TAO. Each subnet produces one thing.
    • Compute: Lium (SN51) rents out GPUs; it billed about $1.3 million in its last full month (as of October 7, 2026), roughly triple a year earlier.
    • Inference: Chutes (SN64) serves open-source models to developers through an API; it reports about 160 billion tokens a day.
    • Training: Covenant-72B, finished March 10, 2026 on Templar (SN3): a 72-billion-parameter model trained by 70+ independent nodes across the open internet.
    • Be fair: the Covenant team left Bittensor in April 2026, calling it "centralized control with decentralized branding", and TAO fell 25 to 30% that day. Worth naming so nobody says you hid it.
The punchline
    • On NEAR, AI runs on the chain. On Bittensor, AI is the product the chain pays for. So the commenter has it backwards: TAO is arguably the more "AI-native" of the two. NEAR is the better "AI-agent economy" bet.

Slide 3 · The Vote and the Fund
On screen: two panels: The Supply Vote (gold, "2.5% → 1.6%") and The Fund (green/red, "+1.1M then -257K"), four points each, then a cyan strip: "TAO has no vote like this. Its supply is set by code."
Vote figures as of October 23, 2026 (read off houseofstake.org); fund holdings as of October 22, 2026 (nrretf.com). If you record on another day, check both pages first and say the live numbers.
The supply vote, talking points
    • HSP-029 on House of Stake, NEAR's governance hub, where holders lock NEAR to get a vote. Proposed by Sal Ternullo (SVRN). It cuts the max new NEAR per year from 2.5% to 1.6%, ramped down over 24 months. Roughly a one-third cut. Opened October 19, closes November 2, 2026.
    • To pass it needs a quorum (35% of the voting power locked in House of Stake, 1.85M veNEAR) and a 67% supermajority.
    • Quorum is already done. Three wallets brought about half of it within hours of opening, and by the evening of October 20 (day 2) the votes passed the quorum. As of October 23: 2.23M veNEAR voted, 21 for, 1 against. The lone "no" is 94 veNEAR, so the yes side is at basically 100%.
    • Why so fast: the whole voting power is only about 0.4% of all NEAR. Seven wallets cast almost all of the 2.23M. A small group sets the supply for everyone else.
    • Who has not shown up: Everstake, one of the big validators, asked for more analysis the week before the vote. It is not in the voter list.
    • Then validators decide. A "yes" still has to be adopted by validators through a network upgrade, with a 90-day grace period before the cut starts. Last time (2025) the House of Stake vote missed its threshold, and the validators passed the 5% to 2.5% cut anyway (Oct 30, 2025).
    • What it means for stakers: staking yield drops from about 5.3% to about 3.4% once fully ramped (The Defiant).
The fund, talking points
    • The Bitwise NEAR ETF (ticker NRR) has traded on NYSE Arca since September 29, 2026. It holds real NEAR, custodied at Coinbase, 0.75% fee.
    • +1.1 million NEAR in one session: 16.5M to 17.61M NEAR on October 19, the day the vote opened. Its biggest single session since the $6 top, about $5.4M, and about 12 days of new supply in one day (the network issues roughly 90,000 new NEAR a day).
    • Then the first outflow. By October 22 the fund held 17.35M NEAR, about 257,000 NEAR less, with shares outstanding down to 3.32M (about 50,000 shares redeemed). Up to the 19th it had not had a single day of outflows since launch. That was the surprise the market was watching for.
    • Bitwise posted in the vote thread that it likes how simple the proposal is and is "approaching this with an open mind". Twist: the fund stakes 100% of its NEAR, so the cut lowers its own yield (gross staking rate 4.94%, per the fund page).
    • The punchline: the fund holds more than 3x the voting power of all of House of Stake, but its wallet is not in the voter list. The biggest buyer of NEAR does not vote on its own supply.
Price context (say it, it is not on the slide)
    • NEAR topped at about $6.00 around October 12. It was about $4.91 when the vote opened on the 19th and is about $4.73 now (Oct 23), roughly 21% off the top. The fund's NAV fell 7.4% on October 21 alone.
    • So far the good governance news has not moved the price: quorum hit fast, near-unanimous yes, and NEAR still drifted lower while the fund saw its first outflow.
    • Bigger picture: NEAR has almost tripled against Bitcoin in 12 months. Against the rest of the altcoin market it is pressing a multi-year resistance line; if the ETF buying keeps slowing, a lot of that run is at risk.
    • Tie it to the commenter: they bought NEAR at about $4.80, right around where the vote opened. Price now is a touch below that.
The TAO contrast (the cyan strip)
    • TAO has no vote like this. Its supply is fixed in code: 21 million cap, Bitcoin-style halvings (the first one in December 2025). Nobody votes on how much new TAO gets printed. NEAR's supply is a governance decision; TAO's is a schedule.

Talking points
    • NEAR is worth more than twice TAO right now ($6.2B vs $2.9B). The market already prices NEAR as the bigger AI coin.
    • Supply is the big difference. TAO copies Bitcoin: 21 million, halvings. NEAR has no cap, but inflation was halved to 2.5% last year and there is a live vote to cut it to 1.6%.
    • Real money: NEAR Intents moved $10.4B in one quarter. TAO subnets bring in $28 to 35 million a year from customers, but that is only about a tenth of what the network pays out in new TAO. So TAO is still subsidized, and the bet is that revenue catches up.
The three questions (walk the cards)
    • 1. Does it produce intelligence, or host it? TAO produces it. NEAR hosts and routes it.
    • 2. Who pays: real customers, or new tokens? Both are early. TAO's customer revenue is ~10% of emissions. NEAR's fees are real but small next to a $6.2B cap.
    • 3. What does the coin capture when usage grows? NEAR: Intents fees fund NEAR buybacks, and staking pays for AI. TAO: every subnet token is priced in TAO, and 14 of the 24 revenue subnets run buybacks.
Your close (your call)
    • If you think the scarce thing is intelligence itself, you want the network that produces it: TAO. If you think the winner is where AI agents live and spend money, that is NEAR. It does not have to be either-or.
