Context

Bitcoin Was Never Meant to Be Perfect

What Satoshi Solved
  • Trustless digital money
  • No central authority needed
  • Censorship resistance
  • Fixed, verifiable supply
  • Permissionless participation
What Was Left Open
  • Transaction throughput
  • Confirmation speed
  • Energy efficiency

Bitcoin solved the hardest problem first. What it left behind opened the door for everything that came next.

The Scalability Problem

~7
Bitcoin transactions per second
24,000+
Visa transactions per second
$50+
Peak average transaction fee (2021/2023)
Why It Happens
  • 1MB block size cap
  • New block every ~10 minutes
  • Limited transactions per block
  • High demand = fee auction
The Real-World Impact
  • Small transfers priced out
  • Not viable for daily payments
  • Congestion during bull markets
  • Hours-long confirmation waits

The First Fix

The Blocksize Wars & Bitcoin Cash

2015–2017
The Debate
Community splits: keep blocks small vs. scale by increasing block size
→
Aug 2017
Bitcoin Cash Forks
Block size raised from 1MB to 8MB. Faster and cheaper transactions
→
Nov 2018
BCH Splits Again
Bitcoin Cash forks into BCH and Bitcoin SV. The community fractures further
What BCH Fixed
  • Faster transactions
  • Lower fees
  • Higher throughput
What BCH Broke
  • Larger blocks need more storage
  • Higher bandwidth to run a node
  • Fewer nodes = more centralization
  • Lost Bitcoin's network effect

Bigger blocks is a blunt instrument. You trade decentralization for speed.

User Experience & Self-Custody

Seed Phrases
  • 12 or 24 random words
  • Must be stored offline
  • Lose it, lose everything
Wallet Addresses
  • 34-character strings
  • One typo = lost funds
  • No name, no confirmation
No Recovery
  • No password reset
  • No customer support
  • No fraud department

Self-sovereignty is powerful. But the burden of security falls entirely on you.

As Money

Volatility & Fungibility

Volatility
  • Price can swing 30%+ in a week
  • Cannot price goods in Bitcoin reliably
  • Not a stable medium of exchange
  • Merchants cannot easily accept it
  • Makes budgeting impossible
Fungibility
  • Blockchain is fully public
  • Every coin has a traceable history
  • Coins linked to illicit activity flagged
  • Exchanges can refuse "tainted" BTC
  • Not all Bitcoin is treated equally

A truly fungible currency treats every unit identically. Bitcoin's transparency works against this.

By Design

What Bitcoin Intentionally Left Out

Bitcoin
Intentionally Simple
  • No native smart contracts
  • No programmable logic
  • Script language is deliberately limited
  • Stability and security over features
The Door It Opened
Ethereum & Beyond
  • Smart contracts on-chain
  • DeFi, NFTs, DAOs
  • Programmable money

Bitcoin's simplicity is a strength and a limitation. It is what makes it secure — and what made the rest of crypto necessary.

The Next Generation

Enter Kaspa

Bitcoin's Base Layer Limits
  • ~7 TPS throughput
  • 10-minute block times
  • Linear blockchain — one block at a time
  • Scaling requires Layer 2 workarounds
Kaspa's BlockDAG Architecture
  • Blocks processed in parallel, not sequence
  • 1 block per second today
  • Roadmap: 10, then 100 blocks per second
  • Near-instant confirmations
  • Fees of fractions of a cent
  • Still proof-of-work

Kaspa doesn't bolt a fix on top. It rebuilds the foundation using the GHOSTDAG protocol — parallel blocks, without sacrificing decentralization.

The Big Picture

Progress Requires Tradeoffs

Bitcoin Cash
  • Traded decentralization for speed
  • Bigger blocks, fewer nodes
  • Lost network effect
  • Largely a failed experiment
Ethereum
  • Traded simplicity for programmability
  • Proof of Stake, not Proof of Work
  • Unlocked DeFi and smart contracts
  • Different trust model
Kaspa
  • Keeps proof-of-work security
  • Solves speed at the base layer
  • blockDAG instead of blockchain
  • Story still being written

Bitcoin's shortcomings were not bugs. They were design choices — and they sparked an entire industry trying to do better.
